Preventing ice dams in New England

If you’ve seen thick ice ridges along your eaves and icicles hanging from the gutters, you’ve seen an ice dam. They’re a New England rite of winter — and a real threat to your roof and walls. The good news: they’re preventable.

Why they form

Warm air leaking into the attic heats the roof deck and melts the snow above it. That meltwater runs down to the cold eaves and refreezes, building a dam. Water then pools behind the dam and works its way under the shingles and into the home.

The three-part fix

1. Air-seal & insulate. Stopping heat from reaching the roof deck keeps it cold and snow from melting unevenly. 2. Ventilate. Balanced soffit-to-ridge airflow keeps the deck temperature consistent. 3. Ice-and-water shield. A waterproof membrane at the eaves and valleys protects the vulnerable areas if a dam does form.

Don’t just chip the ice

Hacking at ice or relying only on roof rakes treats the symptom. Addressing insulation and ventilation treats the cause — and that’s what we build in when we re-roof or insulate.

Questions

Frequently asked

What causes ice dams?+

Heat escaping into the attic melts snow on the upper roof; the water refreezes at the cold eaves, forming a dam that backs water up under the shingles. It’s an insulation and ventilation problem as much as a roofing one.

How do I prevent ice dams for good?+

A combination of air-sealing and insulation (to keep the roof deck cold), proper soffit-to-ridge ventilation, and ice-and-water shield at the eaves. We address all three.
