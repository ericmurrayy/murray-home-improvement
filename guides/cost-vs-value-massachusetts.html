Cost vs. value in the Northeast

Not every remodel returns the same value at resale. Industry “cost vs. value” data consistently shows that curb appeal and mid-range updates tend to recoup the most, while large upscale projects add livability but a smaller percentage return. Here’s how to think about it for a Massachusetts home.

Projects that tend to return the most

Siding & exterior. New siding, trim, and a quality entry door make a striking first impression and recoup strongly. Mid-range kitchens & baths. Thoughtful, broadly-appealing updates beat ultra-custom for ROI. Decks & outdoor living. Usable outdoor space is highly valued in New England’s short summers.

Projects that add livability

Additions, second stories, and high-end kitchens return a smaller percentage of their cost at resale — but they transform how you live in the home, which is often the real goal. If you plan to stay, livability is value.

The local lens

Buyer expectations vary by town and price point. A finish that’s right for one neighborhood may be over- or under-built for another. We’ll help you match the investment to your home and your plans.

Questions

Frequently asked

Which remodels add the most value?+

In the Northeast, exterior projects like siding and a new entry door, along with mid-range kitchen and bath remodels, tend to return the most relative to cost. Major upscale projects return a smaller percentage but add livability.

Should I remodel for resale or for living?+

Both matter. If you’re staying, build for how you live; if you’re selling soon, focus on broadly appealing, mid-range updates and curb appeal.
